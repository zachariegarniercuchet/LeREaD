SUBSTANTIVE ORDER

 

 

1.      On February 19, 2020, the Applicant filed an Application (the “Application”) with the Manitoba Labour Board (the “Board”) seeking a remedy for an alleged unfair labour practice contrary to section 8 of The Labour Relations Act (the “Act”).

2.      On March 30, 2020, following an extension of time, the Respondent, through counsel, filed its Reply requesting that the Board dismiss the Application without a hearing.  The Respondent submitted that the Application was frivolous and vexatious and that it failed to establish a prima facie violation of the Act.  The Respondent also requested that the Applicant be ordered to pay costs and that the Board order her to refrain from filing further frivolous and vexatious applications.

3.      On May 27, 2020, following an extension of time, the Applicant filed a response to the Respondent’s Reply.

4.      The Applicant asserts that the Respondent violated section 8 of the Act.  She claims that the Respondent:  played some role in enforcing a order issued by her former employer, the St. Boniface General Hospital (“SBGH”); threatened petty trespass charges against her; blocked and refused to respond to her correspondence; and prevented her from obtaining employment at workplaces where the Respondent is the bargaining agent.

5.      Section 8 of the Act states:

Discrimination within unions

8  Every union and every person acting on behalf of a union who intimidates or coerces, or who discriminates in regard to employment, a term or condition of employment or membership in a union, against, or imposes a pecuniary or other penalty on, a person who

(a)  has testified or may testify in any proceeding under this or any other Act of the Legislature or of Parliament; or

(b)  has made or may make a disclosure that may be required of him in a proceeding under this or any other Act of the Legislature or of Parliament; or

(c)   has participated in or is about to participate in a proceeding under this or any other Act of the Legislature or of Parliament; or

(d)  has made a complaint or filed an application under this or any other Act of the Legislature or of Parliament; or

(e)  has exercised or is exercising his rights under this or any other Act of the Legislature or of Parliament;

unless he satisfies the board that he did not intimidate or coerce, or discriminate in regard to employment, a term or condition of employment, or membership in a union against, or impose a pecuniary or other penalty on, the person because of any of the reasons set out in clauses (a) to (e), commits an unfair labour practice.

 

6.         Section 8 of the Act is a reverse onus provision.  Before that onus falls upon a “union or any person acting on behalf of the union”, an applicant claiming a violation of section 8 bears the burden of proof to establish a prima facie case.  Specifically, prior to the onus shifting to a respondent under section 8 of the Act, an applicant must establish that:

1)     they were subjected to intimidation or coercion, or discrimination in regard to employment, a term or condition of employment or membership in a union, or they were subjected to a pecuniary or other penalty;

2)     the said action was taken by the union or any person acting on behalf of the union; and

3)     they engaged in one or more of the enumerated activities referred to in clauses (a) to (e) of the provision.

7.     When assessing whether a prima facie case has been established, the Board has consistently indicated that there must be more than a mere allegation or assertion.  Rather, there must be a sufficient factual foundation evident in the application itself in order to enable the Board to draw reasonable conclusions therefrom and which, at a minimum, would call for an answer from the Respondent.

8.     The Applicant has advanced a number of assertions that are unsupported by any factual foundation.  She suggests that the Respondent, or persons acting on its behalf, somehow allowed the SBGH to extend an order banning her from its premises.  The documentation attached to the Application clearly indicates that it was the decision of SBGH to ban the Applicant, not the Respondent.  Similarly, there is no factual foundation to support the Applicant’s allegation that the Respondent, or a person acting on its behalf, threatened her with a petty trespass charge.  The Applicant also alleges, without any factual foundation, that she is “not allowed to work anywhere that UFCW 832 represents”.

9.     The Respondent acknowledges that it blocked all communication with the Applicant upon cessation of her membership having regard to her unreasonable conduct.  This step was taken at a time when there was no further need to communicate with the Applicant as the duty to represent her under a collective agreement no longer existed.

10.  The Applicant has failed to establish that the Respondent, or any person acting on its behalf, acted in a manner prohibited by section 8 of the Act.  Her allegations are mere conjecture, unsupported by any factual foundation.  Furthermore, the Application does not establish any nexus between the actions which she claims were taken against her in relation to the enumerated activities set forth in clauses (a) to (e) of section 8 of the Act.  Accordingly, the Applicant has failed to establish a prima facie case.  The Application is without merit.

11.  The Respondent seeks costs and requests that the Board order the Applicant to “refrain from filing further applications against the Respondent in relation to her previous employment with St. Boniface General Hospital”.

12.  The Board is a statutory tribunal.  It does not have inherent jurisdiction.  The Board does not have the jurisdiction to award costs against an unsuccessful applicant.  Accordingly, the Respondent’s request for an order of costs against the Applicant is dismissed.

13.  However, the Board does have the statutory authority under clause (m) of subsection 142(1) of the Act to “exercise such powers and perform such duties and functions as may be incidental to the powers, duties and functions of the board and the objects of this or any other Act, including the power to make an order requiring compliance with any decision made in respect of a matter before the board”.  As noted in subsection 138(1) of the Act, the Board is responsible for the fair and efficient administration and adjudication of responsibilities assigned to it under the statute.  Persons who persistently institute vexatious proceedings or conduct proceedings in a vexatious manner compromise the ability of the Board to efficiently administer and adjudicate matters before it.  Vexatious applications and conduct also constitute an unwarranted drain on the time and resources of respondents.  The Board has an obligation to ensure that such vexatious applications and conduct are addressed in a manner that minimizes these deleterious effects.

14.  The Board may conclude that a person is engaging in vexatious conduct if they:

a)     commence a proceeding to determine an issue which has already been determined by the Board;

b)     commence a proceeding that cannot possibly succeed, or if no reasonable person can reasonably expect to obtain relief as a result;

c)      commence a proceeding for an improper purpose, including the harassment and oppression of other parties or for purposes other than the assertion of legitimate rights;

d)     repeat and supplement facts and arguments raised in earlier proceedings; or

e)     engage in conduct before the Board which is unreasonable.

15.  The Board agrees with the submission of the Respondent that the Application is frivolous and vexatious.  On its face, there is no possibility that the Application can succeed.  Despite the fact that the Applicant is not an employee under any collective agreement to which the Respondent is a party, she has filed two additional applications against it with this Board.  This Application is an attempt to further harass the Respondent, not to assert legitimate rights.

16.  The Board has the jurisdiction to address the conduct of individuals who persistently, and without reasonable grounds, institute vexatious proceedings.  Pursuant to subsection 140(8) of the Act, “where, in the opinion of the board, a request, application or complaint is without merit or beyond the jurisdiction of the board, it may dismiss the request, application or complaint at any time”.  Where, as here, the Board is satisfied that an individual has instituted a vexatious proceeding, it may elect to exercise its jurisdiction to dismiss any subsequent application that it deems to be vexatious without requiring a response from a respondent.

17.  Accordingly, if the Applicant commences any new proceedings against the Respondent or the Employer, the Board shall undertake an initial review to determine if the proceeding is vexatious.  If the Board determines that the application is, on its face, vexatious and without merit, it will immediately dismiss the application.

18.  That said, the Applicant has a statutory right to review and reconsideration of this Order.  If she files for Review and Reconsideration, the Board will process and consider such application in the normal course.

19.  Having regard to the foregoing, the Board has determined that the Application is without merit and is dismissed pursuant to subsections 140(8) and 30(3)(c) of the Act.

T H E R E F O R E

 

The Manitoba Labour Board HEREBY DISMISSES the Application filed by R.C.A. on February 19, 2020.
