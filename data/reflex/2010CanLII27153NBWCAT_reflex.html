Decision of the Appeals Tribunal:         This appeal is accepted.

 

ISSUE

 

The appellant is appealing the November 6, 2008, decision, upholding the previous decision of August 26, 2008, advising that the Workplace Health, Safety and Compensation Commission (the Commission) of New Brunswick considers the employer’s portion of Registered Retirement Savings Plan (RRSP) withdrawals as remuneration. As a result, his Permanent Physical Impairment (PPI) award was reduced by the amount of $6,109.56 corresponding to the employer’s contributions of his RRSP withdrawal.

RATIONALE/CONCLUSION

 

This information concerning the background which led to the Commission’s decision was taken from the letter of July 21, 2009, addressed to the appellant:

 

… On February 13, 2007 while working in the powder area, you lifted a 20 kg bin up from the scale on the floor to a hopper and felt pain in your lower back. The claim was adjudicated and accepted on February 27, 2007, effective from the date of the accident, and transferred to case management. While in case management you received regular loss of earnings payments, physiotherapy, back surgery, a PPI assessment and a functional capacity evaluation. The functional capacity evaluation identified a work restriction to lifting, carrying and bending and the employer was not able to offer any permanent accommodation. You were referred for vocational rehabilitation services where your skills and interests were evaluated and a plan developed to retrain for a position as a Safety Officer.

 

During the retraining process you continued to receive regular loss of earnings benefits but in August 2008 WorkSafeNB became aware that you had withdrawn money from a registered retirement savings plan (RRSP). Your employer indicated that $12,263.01 was withdrawn from an RRSP plan. WorkSafeNB wrote to you to indicate that the portion of the money contributed by the employer to an RRSP was considered as remuneration and that WorkSafeNB required information as to what percentage of the funds withdrawn were contributed by the employer and what percentage the employee contributed.  The employer provided information that their contribution to the RRSP totaled $6109.56. When WorkSafeNB received this information, it determined that the amount of the overpayment was $6109.56, and advised you that this would be recovered from your PPI award. [sic] [pages 313-314 of the Appeal Record]

 

The appellant does not agree with that and has chosen to file an appeal with the Tribunal.

 

After review of all the information on file and that presented at the hearing, the Appeals Panel concluded that the appellant’s RRSP money should not be considered remuneration and, therefore, the appellant’s PPI award should not have been reduced by the employer’s contribution to that RRSP. The Panel would, therefore, ask the Commission to make appropriate adjustments to reimburse the appellant the amount of $6,109.56 as it relates to the deduction from the PPI award.

 

The Panel provides the following reasons for its decision utilizing mainly these sources: subsection 38.11(9) of the Workers’ Compensation Act (the Act), the Court of Appeal of New Brunswick decision in Roussel-Landry v. Workplace Health, Safety and Compensation Commission, earnings, policy 21-215.

 

1.      Subsection 38.11(9) of the Act states as follows:

 

38.11(9) Notwithstanding subsection (2), where a worker has not received remuneration from the employer or any income replacement or supplement benefit from the employer or from an employment-related source in respect of the injury or recurrence of the injury for a period of time after the injury or recurrence of the injury that is equivalent to three working days and where the worker commences to receive compensation under subsection (2), there shall be payable to the worker only that portion of compensation which, when combined with the amount of any remuneration received by the worker from the employer or any income replacement or supplement benefit received by the worker from the employer or from an employment-related source, does not exceed eighty-five per cent of the worker’s pre-accident net earnings calculated for the same period of time as that during which compensation is paid.

 

The Panel then related the wording of this subsection with the interpretation given of it as found in the Court of Appeal of New Brunswick decision in the Roussel-Landry v. Workplace Health, Safety and Compensation Commission case, which states:

 

 [10]        For the purposes of this case, subsection 38.11(9) provides essentially that when a worker starts receiving compensation from the Commission, the compensation paid, combined with the total of all remuneration received from the employer shall not exceed 85% of the net earnings calculated for the same period for which compensation is paid.

[11]       The employee’s net earnings include all regular remuneration, less applicable taxes, dues and contributions, that the employee was receiving at the time of the injury.  It is agreed that the regular remuneration Ms. Roussel-Landry was receiving at the time of the injury included remuneration for accumulated vacation time and statutory holidays. Moreover, it is common ground that the amount eventually paid was for the accumulated vacation and statutory holidays that Ms. Roussel-Landry was entitled to, and that she was entitled to this remuneration during the same period for which the Commission was paying her compensation equivalent to 85% of her net earnings, i.e., before the end of June 1999.

[12]          The obvious intent in subsection 38.11(9) is to make sure that the amount of compensation payable by the Commission for a given period of time, combined with all other remuneration received from the employer for the same period, does not exceed 85% of the worker’s net earnings before his or her accident.

[13]         In my opinion, the Appeals Tribunal was right when it held that in complying with the Appeals Tribunal’s decision to extend Ms. Roussel-Landry’s period of entitlement to disability benefits, the Commission could and should reduce the amount of benefits owed by the amount she had already received, or equivalent to 31 days, since this was remuneration paid by the employer which consisted in accumulated vacation time and statutory holidays for the same period of time as that during which compensation had been paid by the Commission.  In short, the Appeals Tribunal was right in ruling that subsection 38.11(9) [TRANSLATION] “does not impose a time limit during which the amount [i.e., the remuneration] should or should not be paid” and that the conditions that must be met to allow the Commission’s compensation or benefits to be reduced are the following, namely, that [TRANSLATION] “[remuneration from the employer] be paid . . . and that it be for the same period during which compensation is paid”.

 

The Panel reviewed the pertinent paragraphs of section 38 of the Act along with the Roussel-Landry decision versus the requirements of the Act and concluded that the appellant and the employer had indeed paid into a combined RRSP plan. However, the important point to the Panel was that the contributions were prior to the appellant’s compensable accident, therefore, were not earnings received or given during the time the appellant was receiving the loss of earnings benefits from the Commission. They were in fact savings earned prior to the appellant’s injury. The Panel viewed it as an equivalent to a joint savings account accumulated prior to the accident and not privy to the Commission for deductions in its calculation of benefits. The Panel’s opinion is that the employer’s contribution, which was made to the RRSP plan, was done prior to the accident and, therefore, the Panel’s opinion is that this earning should not be considered as financial remuneration in this case.

 

2. Policy 21-215 Supplement to Compensation, effective at the time of the Commission’s decision, states:

 

1.0   General

 

WorkSafeNB adjudicates all claims using Policy No. 21-100 Conditions for Entitlement – General Principles. Once a claim is accepted, WorkSafeNB provides a range of benefits to injured workers, as required. These benefits may include:

·        Loss of earnings benefits;

·        Lump sum impairment awards;

·        Medical aid; and

·        Rehabilitation services.

 

WorkSafeNB has a legal responsibility to determine an injured worker’s loss of earnings because of a workplace injury. When WorkSafeNB determines that an injured worker is experiencing a loss of earnings due to a temporary or permanent work restriction, WorkSafeNB pays loss of earnings benefits based on the following legislated formula:

Loss of earnings = average net earnings – net estimated capable earnings

 

The compensation payable for new accidents is 85% of loss of earnings. For more information, see Policy No. 21-210 Calculation of Benefits.

 

2.0 Supplements to Compensation

 

Injured workers may also receive remuneration from sources other than WorkSafeNB at the same time they are receiving compensation benefits. The WC Act refers to this as receiving a supplement to compensation.

 

The WC Act requires WorkSafeNB to reduce loss of earnings benefits so that the combination of compensation benefits and remuneration received does not exceed 85% of the injured worker’s pre-accident net earnings.

 

Supplements to compensation include:

·        Any remuneration from the accident employer such as salary, wages and overtime pay;

·        Any income replacement or supplement benefit from the accident employer such as vacation pay, sick leave benefits, and employer sponsored disability benefits; and/or

·        Any income replacement or supplement benefit from the employer and/or an employment-related source such as employer or union retirement pensions and Canada Pension Plan retirement benefits. [pages 370-372 of the Appeal Record]

 

The Panel compared policy 21-215 to subsection 38.11(9) of the Act:

 

38.11(9) Notwithstanding subsection (2), where a worker has not received remuneration from the employer or any income replacement or supplement benefit from the employer or from an employment-related source in respect of the injury or recurrence of the injury for a period of time after the injury or recurrence of the injury that is equivalent to three working days and where the worker commences to receive compensation under subsection (2), there shall be payable to the worker only that portion of compensation which, when combined with the amount of any remuneration received by the worker from the employer or any income replacement or supplement benefit received by the worker from the employer or from an employment-related source, does not exceed eighty-five per cent of the worker’s pre-accident net earnings calculated for the same period of time as that during which compensation is paid. [emphasis added]

 

The Panel noted the words “in respect of the injury or recurrence of the injury”.

 

The Panel concluded that the employer’s contribution to the appellant’s RRSP was not in respect to the injury or recurrence of the injury. Therefore, the employer’s share of the RRSP should not have been deducted as it is not seen as a supplement to compensation or remuneration as related to the appellant’s compensable injury benefits.

 

The Panel examined in detail policy 21-215. It did not find any information that would lead it to conclude that the appellant’s RRSP, specifically the employer’s contribution to it, should in any way affect the calculation of his compensation benefits thus having an overpayment taken from his PPI award.

 

After reviewing all the information on file and that presented at the hearing, the Panel concluded that the employer’s contribution to the appellant’s RRSP money should not be considered remuneration and, therefore, the appellant’s PPI award should not have been reduced by the amount of the overpayment. The Panel would ask that appropriate adjustments be made to reimburse the appellant the amount of $6,109.56 as it relates to deduction from his PPI award.

 

 

 

 

The appeal is, therefore, accepted, and the file is referred to the Commission for appropriate management.
